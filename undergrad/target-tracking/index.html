Target Tracking and Aiming System in Complex Motion Environment

Tianyi Xia
National Second Prize and Regional First Place, RoboMaster University Championship

I was an algorithm developer on my university's RoboMaster team, competing in the RoboMaster University Championship organized by DJI and advancing from the provincial and regional rounds to the national finals. During this time, I:

  • Built the armor recognition pipeline with OpenCV, combining traditional vision with learned object detection, and developed robust monocular and binocular ranging that localizes the target through Perspective-n-Point, bundle-adjustment optimization, and transformations between coordinate frames.
  • Designed target tracking and motion prediction with several Kalman filter variants (KF, EKF, UKF) and their fusion, with strategies tailored to different motion scenarios.
  • Developed localization, navigation, and obstacle avoidance for autonomous robots on ROS with multi-sensor fusion, supporting autonomous movement and decision-making in complex environments.
  • Maintained the codebase, parameter loading and tuning, auto-start scripts, and communication with the control board, and ran algorithm tests to keep the system stable on the field.

Tracking UI

The video below shows our robot's on-board UI while the target armor moves in the X and Y directions at different speeds. The green annotations mean:

  • tracking!: the robot is locked on and tracking the same enemy robot.
  • target_X, target_Y, target_Z: the armor center in world coordinates after coordinate conversion.
  • final_yaw, final_pitch: the solved yaw and pitch toward the target in world coordinates.
  • Armor Type: 0 for small armor, 1 for large armor.
  • no gyro!: the target is not spinning in place (“gyro” mode).
  • dir: the direction the target is moving in the image, e.g. right.
  • bullet_speed: the robot's set projectile speed.
  • msg_yaw: the yaw command sent to the control board.
  • Points on the armor: green is the current center, red is the predicted center at a future time step, and the circle is the predicted landing point of the projectile.
  • A white cross on an armor marks a discarded target.
